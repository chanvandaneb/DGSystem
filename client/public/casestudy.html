DGSystem — Workforce Management Dashboard

Designing a unified internal operations platform that replaced five disconnected tools with one cohesive, accessible desktop interface for a creative design team.

The Problem

An 8-person creative team managed daily operations across spreadsheets, Telegram threads, and informal processes. There was no single interface where a team member could check their own attendance, see task status, or find shared credentials.

Managers had to manually compile KPI reports, task progress lived in group chats that scrolled away, and new team members didn't know where to look for anything. The experience was fragmented, error-prone, and frustrating.

  • Team members couldn't check their own attendance without contacting HR.
  • KPI scores were calculated manually from spreadsheets — often late or inaccurate.
  • Task assignments lived in group chats that scrolled away and got missed.
  • Shared passwords were sent over chat with no central record.
  • Announcements pinned in Telegram got buried under daily conversation.

Design question

How do you design one consistent interface that makes every team member's daily workflow feel effortless — regardless of their role?

Define & Synthesis

After mapping the research findings, I reframed the problems as How Might We statements to open up the design space before committing to solutions.

HMW

How might we let team members check their own attendance without contacting HR?

→ Led to: Self-serve calendar heatmap per employee

HMW

How might we make KPI scores visible and fair without a manager manually compiling them?

→ Led to: Leaderboard with approval workflow

HMW

How might we surface task updates without relying on group chats that scroll away?

→ Led to: Persistent task board with due-date indicators

HMW

How might we give the manager a morning overview of the team's status in under 30 seconds?

→ Led to: Dashboard with live clock, presence, task ring

Dashboard first

It's the daily entry point. Clock, KPI snapshot, task ring, and team presence give instant orientation without navigating anywhere.

Sidebar always visible

Permanent sidebar means zero "where am I?" confusion. The team works at desks — hiding nav on desktop costs too much.

Workforce grouped together

Attendance, KPI, Tasks, and Accounts are all job-related. Grouping them tells users: this is where your daily work lives.

Role-based visibility

Non-admins see only their own KPI entries. Admins see the full leaderboard and approve/reject columns. Same layout, different data.

Testing & Iteration

I ran informal usability walkthroughs with 3 team members across two rounds, focusing on the most-used screens: attendance, tasks, and the dashboard.

Round 1 — Issues Found

Attendance calendar: month unclear

"I don't know which month this is showing without scrolling up."

KPI page: approve button not visible to non-admins

"I see the entries but where do I approve them?"

Tasks: no way to tell what's overdue

"These all look the same — I can't tell which ones are late."

What changed after testing

→

Added month + year header above attendance calendar

Testers didn't know which period was showing without context.

→

Approve/Reject column only visible to admins

Non-admins were confused by review buttons they had no permission to use.

→

Colour-coded due-date badges (red = overdue, amber = today)

Tasks were visually identical — urgency was invisible at a glance.

→

Added sidebar collapse to give content more room

On smaller laptops the sidebar was eating too much horizontal space.

Reflection & Learnings

What worked well

  • ✓Desktop-first layout with a persistent sidebar eliminated "lost in navigation" moments entirely.
  • ✓Colour-coded attendance heatmap makes punctuality trends instantly readable without needing numbers.
  • ✓Separating KPI entry from leaderboard kept the privacy model clear and simple.
  • ✓Consistent card padding across all 9 modules made the interface feel like one product, not a patchwork.

What I'd do differently

  • →Run usability testing with the actual team earlier — some edge cases in the attendance calendar were found late.
  • →Design a mobile companion view — team members sometimes check their status on phones between meetings.
  • →Add empty states with clear CTAs — some first-time screens feel blank without seed data context.
  • →Explore a notification system so announcement updates surface even when the app isn't open.
